Identity: find tradable small-cap opportunities that SPY doesn't capture. Record: 1 trade in 7 weeks. KD was a Tier 3 starter: bought at 17.49, sold at 17.13 on its time stop, about -2%, -$1.03. We're at -0.30% since start and SPY is at -3.01%. That lead comes from sitting in cash while SPY fell, not from edge. In up weeks, cash lags (this week +0.46% vs SPY +2.18%).

## What the data looks like

## Screening
- Drop warrants, units, rights, leveraged or inverse ETFs, anything under $0.50, volume under $1M, spreads over 1.5%, and moves of ±40% or more.
- Tier 1: names moving ±3–15% with a spread of 1.5% or less and real volume.
- Tier 2 (watchlist): liquid names moving 15–40%. Buy only on day-2 continuation or a stabilized 30–50% pullback. Write the name down. If it isn't visible the next day, drop it.
- Tier 3 (day-1 starter, max 1 per week): an up-move of 15–35%, spread ≤0.5%, volume ≥$10M, and a news catalyst. Put the limit 3–5% below the close; KD filled at full price and went straight down. Confidence 3, smallest size. Stop at the midpoint between the prior close and entry. Target 2x risk. Max hold 3 sessions.
- No new entries on days SPY is down 2% or more.

## Entry and sizing
- Confidence 3–4, max 2 names, limit orders only. Don't chase.
- The expected move must be at least 3x the spread.

## Exits
- Set the stop, target and time stop before buying, and follow them mechanically. The KD time stop worked: it capped the loss at about 2% without extending the hold. Keep doing this.
- Don't hold through binary events.

## Confidence
- Tournament-wide buys: 1–4 averaged -0.13% (n=6), 5–7 averaged -4.23% (n=13), 8–10 averaged -6.25% (n=2). That makes 3 weeks in a row where higher confidence went with worse results.
- Default is 3. Use 5 only with a fresh catalyst, spread ≤1%, and a clean chart. Never use 6 or higher.
