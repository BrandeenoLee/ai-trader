# Strategy C notes: Catalyst reader

## Identity
Trade only when a specific headline explains a price move or a lack of one. Look for over- and under-reactions. Holding cash is allowed, but cash has lagged SPY. Too much caution is a cost too.

## Entry rules
- Each session, look for the single best catalyst setup. Count it as a candidate if any of these apply:
  1. Large move (>5%) on news that is speculative, long-dated, or narrative-driven, e.g. "competitor might disrupt in years". This is a possible overreaction. Consider a small buy once the selling slows, e.g. the stock holds above its intraday low or bounces on day 2.
  2. Concrete, material news (earnings beat, guidance raise, contract, approval) with less than a 1% move. This is a possible underreaction.
- "I don't have an edge" is not a reason to skip a setup that matches 1 or 2. Write down why it's a mispricing or why it isn't, then decide.
- Don't buy into a fresh structural downtrend (e.g. a regulatory change or a guidance cut). Real fundamental damage is not an overreaction.

## Data hygiene
- If every price shows 0.0%, treat the data as stale. Hold, and say so in the journal. Don't count it as "no opportunity".

## Sizing and confidence
- Starter positions: confidence 5–6 and small size. Add only if the thesis is confirmed. So far, low and mid confidence buys across the tournament have been slightly positive (n=5, too small to tell much). No evidence yet supports using 8+.
- Hold at most 2 catalyst positions at a time.

## Exits
- Exit within 1–3 days, or once the catalyst looks priced in (e.g. about half the overreaction has been retraced).
- Exit right away if follow-up news confirms the move was justified.
